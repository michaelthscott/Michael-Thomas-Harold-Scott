My mother's father, Thomas Francis Ord 
Rippingham, already retired when I knew 
him, was a quiet, chess playing English 
architect, who had come to Ulster in the 
late 1920s to work as a government architect.

My father's, Harold Taggart Scott, was amore lively character, given 
to doing a conjuring act at children's parties. 

As a young man he had been a mechanic, working 
according to family legend in a motor repair 
shop where at some time Dunlop, inventor of the
phumatic bicycle wheel, had worked. 

His garage was well stocked with tools and 
jars full of screws. He was a fisherman, golfer 
and a fine figure on the bowling green.
